Medical Technologies Corporation (MTC)

A Supply Chain Strategy to Recover Net Income

Rutgers Supply Chain Management Strategy — Coursera. October 07, 2026

1. The problem

  • Net income fell $12.8M (from $15.1M to $2.3M) despite stable sales
  • Root causes traceable to the supply chain: expedited freight, inventory write-offs, poor forecast accuracy, and reactive outsourcing decisions
  • Siloed functions: logistics, operations, planning and sourcing each optimize locally

2. Logistics & Operations fixes

  • Network redesign: close the losing DC and re-balance to 2 regional DCs chosen with the center-of-gravity method (savings est. $1.1M/yr in freight)
  • Warehouse layout: ABC slotting to cut travel time ~30%
  • Lean/quality: DMAIC on sterilization rework; target 40% defect reduction

3. Planning & Sourcing fixes

  • Forecasting: replace naive forecasts with exponential smoothing (alpha tuned by MAD), lifting forecast accuracy and cutting safety stock
  • S&OP: monthly cross-functional planning meeting to align demand and supply
  • Sourcing: make-versus-buy on sterilization with weighted supplier scorecards; dual-sourcing for critical SKUs

4. Expected impact

Freight & warehousing: +$1.5M net income recovery
Inventory carrying cost reduction (60-day to 45-day cover): +$0.9M
Rework and expedite elimination: +$2.2M
Total modeled recovery: ~$4.6M of the $12.8M gap in year 1, with the rest following as forecast accuracy compounds

5. Governance

  • One supply chain strategy owner reporting to the COO
  • Monthly S&OP + quarterly strategy review
  • Shared KPIs: forecast accuracy (MAPE), OTIF, inventory turns, cost-to-serve
